# Long-Distance and Relocation Drafting Reference

De-identified structural patterns distilled from the firm's executed and proposed long-distance Parenting Plans. All names, addresses, case numbers, and identifying details have been removed. Use this as the pattern guide the moment the case involves parents in different regions, a pending or anticipated relocation, or a § 61.13001 posture. Do not force a local alternating-week structure onto a long-distance case.

## When Long-Distance Logic Applies

- Parents live (or will live) far enough apart that regular midweek or alternating-weekend exchanges are impractical (commonly out-of-state, or beyond roughly a 50-mile / one-hour band).
- One parent has filed, or is expected to file, a Petition to Relocate under Fla. Stat. § 61.13001.
- A military PCS or job transfer will move a parent out of the area (see also special-schedules.md Part 3 for the dual-track local/long-distance trigger clause).

## Relocation Law Framing (Fla. Stat. § 61.13001)

If a parent intends to move more than 50 miles from their current residence for 60 consecutive days or more, they need either (a) a signed written agreement from the other parent (with a time-sharing schedule and transportation arrangements) or (b) a Petition to Relocate served on the other parent, who has 20 days to object. The petition must state the new address, the reasons for relocating, and a proposed post-relocation (long-distance) parenting plan. The court decides based on the child's best interests. Draft relocation compliance language into every plan; use the restricted-radius or mediation-first options from standard-provisions.md / high-conflict-provisions.md when the facts warrant.

## Long-Distance Plan Section Architecture

Follow the standard skeleton (plan-skeleton.md) but expect these sections to carry the weight:

1. **School-year residence and school designation.** Identify which parent's residence controls for school purposes and which county's school calendar governs (firm pattern: "the parents shall follow the school calendar of [the school-parent's] county of residence," obtained by both parents on or before July 1 each year).
2. **Regular schedule during the school year.** Typically majority time with the school-parent; the distant parent's regular time is limited (e.g., alternating or monthly weekends where distance permits, or none, with make-up concentrated in breaks). Do not default to equal timesharing.
3. **Break-based blocks for the distant parent.** The core of the plan:
   - **Winter Break:** split into two defined periods with exact start/end dates and times, alternating annually (common firm model: Period 1 from school dismissal through a fixed mid-break date/time; Period 2 through the day before school resumes; the Thanksgiving parent takes Period 2).
   - **Spring Break:** alternate the entire break by even/odd years.
   - **Thanksgiving:** either alternate the long weekend or leave with the regular-schedule parent, linked to winter-break allocation.
   - **Summer:** the distant parent takes the majority or a large defined share. Firm models include: alternating two-week blocks across the summer; a single extended block (e.g., 4-6 weeks) with written week-selection deadlines and alternating selection priority; or the distant parent taking all but the first and last weeks. Always include a selection deadline (e.g., written notice by April 1/May 1) and a default if no notice is given.
   - **Long weekends / teacher workdays:** optionally assign three-day school weekends to the distant parent when they are already in the area, with notice.
4. **Overnight count.** State each parent's annual overnights; the two numbers must total 365.
5. **Transportation and exchange logistics.** Specify who books and pays for travel (equal split, proportional to income, or shifted to the relocating parent), the exchange location(s) with exact addresses or "a mutually agreed midpoint," whether an adult must accompany air travel and to what age, unaccompanied-minor procedures, and a lateness rule (firm pattern: more than 60 minutes late without contact, the other parent may proceed with plans).
6. **Travel notice and itineraries.** Out-of-state: written notice (commonly 7-14 days) with detailed itinerary, locations, and phone numbers. International: longer notice (commonly 30 days), written consent requirement, itinerary, passport-control language, and — where abduction or enforcement risk exists — travel limited to Hague Convention signatory countries. For non-Hague or accession-only countries, do NOT rely on generic "non-Hague country" language; verify the specific country's enforcement relationship with the United States and draft country-specific protective language (surrender of passports, bond, prohibition) as attorney-flagged options.
7. **Parent-child communication.** Defined call/video windows during the other parent's time (firm pattern: daily or defined evening window, e.g., 6:00-8:00 p.m.), device access rules, no unreasonable monitoring or interruption, cost allocation for the child's phone, and time-zone adjustment where applicable.
8. **Relocation compliance clause.** Statutory-standard clause plus any negotiated radius, reversion clause (if the distant parent moves back within the radius, the local schedule resumes), and mediation-first requirements.

## Drafting Cautions

- Exact dates and times everywhere. "Half of winter break" without dismissal/return anchors generates litigation.
- Tie holiday precedence explicitly: holiday schedule > break/vacation schedule > regular schedule.
- Anticipate cost fights: who pays for flights, what happens when a flight is missed or canceled, and reimbursement mechanics with documentation deadlines.
- If relocation is contested and pending, label the plan clearly as the proposed long-distance plan and keep the pre-relocation schedule intact as the operative fallback.
