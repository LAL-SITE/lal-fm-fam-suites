# Firm Drafting Tips and Judgment Rules

Distilled from the firm's Parenting Plan Drafting Guide for Paralegals and drafting workbook. These are judgment rules, not clauses.

## What Every Plan Must Include
Time-sharing schedule (regular, holidays, summer, breaks); parental responsibility (shared vs. sole, decision-making authority); communication rules (between parents, with child); transportation and exchanges; medical, education, and extracurricular provisions.

## Conflict Calibration
- **Low conflict:** flexibility clauses ("as mutually agreed") are acceptable — but always pair them with a stated default so the plan is self-executing if cooperation breaks down.
- **High conflict:** black-and-white terms only. No wiggle room. South Florida judges expect detailed plans in high-conflict cases; vague language like "parents will share holidays equally" produces return trips to court. Be specific: times, dates, locations, transportation responsibilities.
- Anticipate flashpoints (holidays, travel, religion) instead of leaving gaps that fuel motions. Winter break in particular consumes hours of mediation over cultural and family obligations — always list exact start/end date-times.

## Special Provisions to Watch For
- Screen time and electronics rules where the facts raise them.
- Parenting communication apps: firm default is TalkingParents; always specify the method of communication and, in controlled cases, response deadlines and deemed-waiver/proceed-if-no-response language.
- Medical authority: who schedules appointments, how information is shared.
- Special-needs children: therapy schedules, sensory needs, unique routines; tie every provision back to the child's best interest.

## Work and Lifestyle Realities
Nurses, firefighters, and shift workers need nontraditional schedules — standard alternating weekends won't work. Draft to the actual rotation ("parent works 48 on / 72 off, so exchanges occur on the first day off"). Be realistic: if a parent claims they want equal time but works nights, the plan must reflect actual caregiving availability. Judges see through aspirational plans. See special-schedules.md.

## Drafting for Longevity
Parenting plans can only be modified through a Supplemental Petition — draft with the child's aging, school changes, and long-term feasibility in mind. A plan built for a toddler (short frequent visits) needs a built-in review or step-up clause for school age.

## Communication and Dispute Resolution
Specify approved methods; include mediation-before-litigation. High-conflict co-parents who cannot exchange civilly may need neutral or no-contact exchange sites (public locations, police station, supervised exchange centers).

## Confidentiality
If a protected-address, injunction, or domestic-violence issue appears anywhere in the materials, do not insert a protected address just because the form has a blank. Use the confidential-address recital from the master library Section I and flag for attorney confirmation.

## Firm Non-Negotiables
- Holiday schedule takes precedence over vacation; vacation takes precedence over the regular schedule.
- Overnight totals must sum to 365.
- Do not default to equal timesharing or shared parental responsibility unless the facts support it.
- Every draft is attorney-reviewed before filing; unresolved forks are presented as clean Option A / Option B, never as blanks buried in the text.
